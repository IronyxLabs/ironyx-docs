= Domain model

The Ironyx domain model describes the core concepts of the platform and how they relate across the SDLC.

NOTE: This document is a high-level map; detailed definitions live under '/product/spec/domain'

.Defined domain models:
- *Product:* The primary container for all work, context, decisions, and artifacts across the entire lifecycle of a long‑lived system.  
- *Domain Area:* A logically bounded business domain within a Product that groups related features, entities, workflows, and responsibilities under a shared context.
- *Initiative:* A time‑bounded development effort within a Product.
Represents a concrete goal, may span multiple Domain Areas, and aggregates several features.
- *Milestone:* A time‑based checkpoint or achieved state within the Product roadmap.
Represents an outcome, not the work itself.  
- *Roadmap item:* A planned, higher‑level strategic theme or direction within the Product.
Roadmap items break down into Initiatives.  
- *Decision:* A recorded architectural, business, domain, UX, or organizational decision with rationale and impact on the Product.
- *Document:* Any attached or authored document providing additional context (specifications, RFCs, notes, research).

== Product model

Describes the top-level structure of work in Ironyx.

A Product is the primary container for all work, context, decisions, and artifacts across the entire lifecycle of a long‑lived system.
It defines the strategic direction, domain boundaries, roadmap, and release lifecycle of the platform.

.A Product includes:
* Domain Areas
* Initiatives
* Milestones
* Roadmap items
* Decisions
* Documents
* Backlog and feature definitions

A Product is stable, long‑lived, and evolves continuously.

== Domain Area model
A Domain Area is a logically bounded business domain within a Product.
It groups related features, entities, workflows, and responsibilities under a shared context.

.A Domain Area defines:
* *Purpose* — why this domain exists
* *Scope* — what belongs inside the domain
* *Entities* — core business objects
* *Workflows* — how the domain behaves
* *Features* — user‑facing capabilities
* *Rules & invariants* — domain constraints
* *Decisions* — domain‑specific choices
* *Cross‑domain relationships* — interactions with other areas

Every Feature belongs to exactly one Domain Area.
Domain Areas evolve with the Product and form its internal structure.

== Initiative model
An Initiative is a time‑bounded development effort within a Product.
It represents a concrete goal and may span multiple Domain Areas.

.An Initiative:
* aggregates multiple Features
* produces one or more Milestones
* is aligned with a Roadmap item
* may introduce new Decisions
* has a clear scope and outcome

Initiatives represent the work, not the long‑term structure.

== Milestone model
A Milestone is a time‑based checkpoint or achieved state within the Product roadmap.
It represents an outcome, not the work itself.

.Milestones:
* mark progress
* validate Initiative completion
* are used for planning and release management
* do not contain features or tasks

Examples:
“MMP delivered”, “Beta ready”, “v1.0 shipped”.

== Roadmap item model
A Roadmap item is a planned, higher‑level strategic theme or direction within a Product.

.Roadmap items:
* express long‑term goals
* break down into Initiatives
* may span multiple quarters or releases
* define the evolution of the Product

Examples:
“Permission Model Expansion”, “Billing Platform”, “Observability Foundation”.

== Decision model
A Decision is a recorded architectural, business, domain, UX, or organizational choice with rationale and impact.

.A Decision includes:
* the question or problem
* available options
* analysis and trade‑offs
* the chosen option
* rationale
* consequences
* ownership (role‑based)

.Decision types include:
* Technical
* Business
* Domain Model
* UX
* Organizational

Decisions ensure long‑term consistency across the Product.

== Document model
A Document is any authored or attached artifact that provides additional context.

.Document types include:
* Specifications
* RFCs
* Notes
* Research
* Architecture descriptions
* Diagrams
* Meeting summaries

Documents support clarity, onboarding, and decision‑making across the Product.
